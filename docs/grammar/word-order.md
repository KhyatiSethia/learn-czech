# Word order, questions and negation

Because the endings show who does what to whom, Czech word order is flexible. It is not random, though. It follows two principles: **old information first, new information last**, and **short weak words in second position**.

## Neutral order: subject, verb, the rest

The basic order is the same as in English: *Petr čte knihu.* (Petr is reading a book.) What changes is the *emphasis*. The new or important part of the message goes at the **end**:

| Czech | What it stresses |
| --- | --- |
| *Na stole je **kniha**.* | There's a **book** on the table (new information). |
| *Kniha je na **stole**.* | The book is on the **table** (the book is known, the place is new). |
| *Pes kousl **chlapce**.* | The dog bit the **boy**. |
| *Chlapce kousl **pes**.* | It was a **dog** that bit the boy. |

Czech has no *the* or *a*, so word order does some of that work: a known thing comes early, a new thing comes late.

## Second position: the clitics

Some short, unstressed words must stand in **second position**: after the first word or the first phrase of the clause. They are:

1. the auxiliary verbs *jsem, jsi, jsme, jste* and *bych, bys, by, bychom, byste*
2. the reflexives *se* and *si*
3. short dative pronouns: *mi, ti, mu, jí, nám, vám, jim*
4. short accusative pronouns: *mě, tě, ho, ji, nás, vás, je*, and *to*

They always keep this **order**: auxiliary, *se/si*, dative, accusative.

| Czech | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| *Včera **jsem se** vrátil domů.* | I came home yesterday. |
| *Dal **bych si** kávu.* | I'd have a coffee. |
| *Koupil **jsem mu to**.* | I bought it for him. |
| *Dám **ti to** zítra.* | I'll give it to you tomorrow. |
| *Líbí **se mi** to.* | I like it. |
| *Řekl **jsem jí to**.* | I told her that. |

Think of the second position as a fixed slot, like a parking space. Even when the pronoun belongs to a verb later in the sentence, it moves up to the slot:

- *Chci **se** učit česky.* (not ~~*Chci učit se česky*~~) I want to study Czech.
- *Musím **se** vrátit domů.* I have to go back home.
- *Chtěl **bych se** zeptat.* I'd like to ask.

### What counts as "first position"

A clitic can follow one word or a whole phrase that belongs together:

- *Dnes **se** učím.* (Dnes = first word)
- *Můj starší bratr **se** učí.* (a whole noun phrase = first position)
- *Protože **jsem** byl unavený…* (the conjunction, then the clitic)
- *Řekl, že **se** vrátí.* ("že" counts as the first word of the subordinate clause)

Don't start a clause with *se* or *jsem* in the past tense: say *Byl jsem doma*, never ~~*Jsem byl doma*~~. But the present tense *jsem* is a full verb and can come first: *Jsem unavený.*

## Questions

### Yes/no questions

The word order does **not** change. You change the intonation, with a rise at the end, and in writing you add a question mark:

- *Máš čas?* Do you have time?
- *Mluvíte anglicky?* Do you speak English?
- *Je to daleko?* Is it far?

To invite agreement, add *že*: *To je dobré, že?* *(That's good, isn't it?)*

### Question words

The question word goes first:

| Czech | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| *kdo* | who |
| *co* | what |
| *kde* | where |
| *kam* | where to |
| *odkud* | where from |
| *kdy* | when |
| *jak* | how |
| *proč* | why |
| *kolik* | how many, how much |
| *který, která, které* | which |
| *čí* | whose |

*Kde bydlíš?* *Kam jdeš?* *Odkud jsi?* *Kdy přijedeš?* *Proč se učíš česky?* *Jak se jmenuješ?*

*Kdo* takes a masculine singular verb, even if the answer is a group of people or a woman: *Kdo to byl?* *Kdo přišel?*

## Negation

Add **ne-** to the verb and write it as one word: *nemám*, *nejde*, *nevím*. Czech also uses **negative words** in the same sentence, and the verb must be negated too. This is a *double negative*, and it is required:

| Positive | Negative |
| --- | --- |
| *někdo* someone | *nikdo* nobody |
| *něco* something | *nic* nothing |
| *někdy* sometimes | *nikdy* never |
| *někde* somewhere | *nikde* nowhere |
| *někam* to somewhere | *nikam* to nowhere |
| *nějaký* some | *žádný* no, none |

- *Nikdo nic neví.* Nobody knows anything.
- *Nikdy tam nechodím.* I never go there.
- *Nikde ho nevidím.* I can't see him anywhere.
- *Nemám žádný problém.* I have no problem.

## Commas

Czech punctuation is strict:

- Put a comma before every **subordinate clause**: *Myslím, že máš pravdu.* *Nevím, jestli přijde.* *Chci, abys přišel.*
- A comma goes before *ale* (but): *Chci jít, ale nemám čas.*
- There is usually no comma before *a* or *nebo*.
- Czech writes numbers with a decimal **comma** (*3,5*) and a **space** for thousands (*10 000*).
