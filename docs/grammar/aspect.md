# Aspect: verb pairs

Most Czech verbs come in **pairs**. One is *imperfective* (the action as a process, a habit or a state), the other *perfective* (the action seen as finished, with a result). This is the biggest difference from English verb thinking, so it pays to learn verbs as pairs.

| | Imperfective | Perfective |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Question it answers | What were you doing? | What did you get done? |
| Focus | process, repeating, duration | result, one completed event |
| Past | *psal jsem* I was writing, I used to write | *napsal jsem* I wrote it (it's done) |
| Present | *píšu* I write, I'm writing | no real present tense |
| Future | *budu psát* I'll be writing | *napíšu* I'll write it (and finish) |

## How the pairs are made

1. **A prefix** makes the perfective: *dělat → **u**dělat*, *psát → **na**psat*, *číst → **pře**číst*, *platit → **za**platit*.
2. **A different ending** gives the imperfective partner: *koupit → kupovat*, *vrátit se → vracet se*, *otevřít → otevírat*.
3. **A different verb** altogether: *brát / vzít*, *říkat / říct*.

Some verbs have no pair, such as *být* and *mít*.

## Which one should I use?

**Imperfective** when you talk about:

- a habit or repetition: *Každý den píšu deník.* I write a diary every day.
- how long something took: *Dělal jsem to hodinu.* I was doing it for an hour.
- an action in progress: *Co děláš?* What are you doing?
- starting, stopping or continuing: *Začal jsem psát.* *Přestala kouřit.* (After *začít*, *přestat* and *pokračovat*, the infinitive is imperfective.)

**Perfective** when you talk about:

- one finished action with a result: *Napsal jsem e-mail.* I've written the e-mail.
- doing something within a time limit: *Udělal jsem to za hodinu.* I did it in an hour.
- a sudden or completed event: *Najednou zazvonil telefon.* Suddenly the phone rang.
- "Have you already…?": *Už jsi to přečetl?* Have you read it already?

Useful clues: *každý den, často, vždycky, celý den, dlouho* point to the imperfective. *Najednou, hned, už, konečně* point to the perfective.

## Aspect and the future

| | Imperfective | Perfective |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Form | *budu* + infinitive | present-tense endings |
| Example | *Zítra budu psát.* Tomorrow I'll be writing. | *Zítra napíšu.* Tomorrow I'll write it. |

The perfective form looks like the present tense but means the future: *Zavolám ti večer.* I'll call you in the evening.

## Aspect and the imperative

A positive request can use either, but the *negative* imperative is almost always imperfective: *Udělej to hned.* (Do it now.) but *Nedělej to.* (Don't do it.) A negative perfective is a warning about an accident: *Nezapomeň!* (Don't forget!)

## Verbs of motion

*Jít* (to go on foot) and *jet* (to go by vehicle) are imperfective. A prefix turns them into perfective verbs with a direction: *přijít* (to arrive), *odejít* (to leave), *vejít* (to go in), *vyjít* (to go out). Their imperfective partners are *přicházet*, *odcházet*.
