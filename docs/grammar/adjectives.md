# Adjectives

Adjectives come before the noun and agree with it in **gender, number and case**. That means an adjective has as many forms as a noun has cases, so it helps that they follow just two main patterns: hard (*nový* new) and soft (*cizí* foreign).

*nový dům*, *nová kniha*, *nové auto*  
*Bydlím v malém bytě.* I live in a small flat. (masculine, locative)  
*Vidím velkého psa.* I see a big dog. (masculine animate, accusative)  
*Jedu s mým novým kamarádem.* I'm going with my new friend. (masculine, instrumental)

## Hard adjectives: *nový*

### Singular

| Case | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nominative | nov**ý** | nov**á** | nov**é** |
| Genitive | nov**ého** | nov**é** | nov**ého** |
| Dative | nov**ému** | nov**é** | nov**ému** |
| Accusative | nov**ého** (animate) / nov**ý** (inanimate) | nov**ou** | nov**é** |
| Vocative | nov**ý** | nov**á** | nov**é** |
| Locative | nov**ém** | nov**é** | nov**ém** |
| Instrumental | nov**ým** | nov**ou** | nov**ým** |

### Plural

| Case | Masculine animate | Masculine inanimate | Feminine | Neuter |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nominative | nov**í** | nov**é** | nov**é** | nov**á** |
| Genitive | nov**ých** | nov**ých** | nov**ých** | nov**ých** |
| Dative | nov**ým** | nov**ým** | nov**ým** | nov**ým** |
| Accusative | nov**é** | nov**é** | nov**é** | nov**á** |
| Vocative | nov**í** | nov**é** | nov**é** | nov**á** |
| Locative | nov**ých** | nov**ých** | nov**ých** | nov**ých** |
| Instrumental | nov**ými** | nov**ými** | nov**ými** | nov**ými** |

Notice how little changes in the plural: only the nominative, vocative and accusative still care about gender.

## Soft adjectives: *cizí*

Adjectives whose stem is soft end in *-í* and barely change at all.

| Case | Singular, all genders | Plural, all genders |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nominative | ciz**í** | ciz**í** |
| Genitive | ciz**ího** (m., n.) / ciz**í** (f.) | ciz**ích** |
| Dative | ciz**ímu** (m., n.) / ciz**í** (f.) | ciz**ím** |
| Accusative | ciz**ího** (m. anim.) / ciz**í** (others) | ciz**í** |
| Locative | ciz**ím** (m., n.) / ciz**í** (f.) | ciz**ích** |
| Instrumental | ciz**ím** (m., n.) / ciz**í** (f.) | ciz**ími** |

Other soft adjectives: *moderní* (modern), *domácí* (home, domestic), *první* (first), *poslední* (last).

## Adjectives that look like possessives

Possessive adjectives in *-ův* and *-in* take adjective-like endings and name a single owner: *Petrův dům* (Petr's house), *Annina kniha* (Anna's book), *otcův stůl* (father's desk).

## Adjective or adverb?

Most adverbs are made from the adjective with a different ending:

| Adjective | Adverb | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| dobr**ý** | dob**ře** | good, well |
| špatn**ý** | špatn**ě** | bad, badly |
| rychl**ý** | rychl**e** | quick, quickly |
| krásn**ý** | krásn**ě** | beautiful, beautifully |
| pomal**ý** | pomal**u** | slow, slowly |
| hezk**ý** | hezk**y** | pretty, prettily |

Adjectives in *-ský* give the adverb in *-sky*, which doubles as the way to name a language: *český* (Czech) → *Mluvím česky.* (I speak Czech); *anglicky* (in English).

## Adjectives after *být*

When the adjective follows *být* it agrees with the subject in gender and number, and it is in the nominative: *Praha je krásná.* (Prague is beautiful.) *Domy jsou staré.* (The houses are old.)
