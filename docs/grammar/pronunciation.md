# Sounds and spelling

Czech spelling is close to phonetic: once you know the letters, you can read almost any word aloud. The catch is that a few marks change the sound a lot.

## The marks

| Mark | Name | What it does | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ´ | čárka | Makes a vowel long | *á é í ó ú ý* |
| ˇ | háček | Changes the consonant | *č ř š ž ď ť ň ě* |
| ˚ | kroužek | A long *u*, written inside words | *ů* |

Czech people consider the marks part of the letter. Leaving them out changes the word, so the exercises here treat a missing mark as a near miss (or as a mistake in strict mode).

## Vowels: short and long

The vowels are *a e i o u y*. Each has a long partner marked with a čárka: *á é í ó ú/ů ý*. Length is not stress, it is how long you hold the sound. It can change the meaning:

| Short | Long |
| --- | --- |
| *dal* (he gave) | *dál* (further) |
| *byt* (flat) | *být* (to be) |

Two long *u* letters exist. *Ú* appears at the start of a word or after a prefix (*úkol* task, *úřad* office). *Ů* is used inside words (*dům* house, *kůň* horse) and never starts a word.

*I* and *y* sound the same. The difference is in the spelling and in what they do to the consonant before them (see below).

## Consonants

| Letter | Sound | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| c | *ts* as in cats | *cesta* (road) |
| č | *ch* as in church | *čaj* (tea) |
| š | *sh* | *škola* (school) |
| ž | like the *s* in measure | *žena* (woman) |
| j | *y* as in yes | *jméno* (name) |
| ch | like *ch* in Scottish *loch*; counts as one letter | *chleba* (bread) |
| h | a soft, voiced *h*, never silent | *hora* (mountain) |
| ř | see below | *řeka* (river) |

### The letter ř

*Ř* is the sound Czech is known for. Roll an *r* and buzz a *ž* at the same moment. Next to voiceless sounds it loses its voice and sounds closer to *rsh*, as in *tři* (three) and *přítel* (friend). Practise with *dobře* (well), *spořit* (to save), *řeka*.

### Soft d, t, n

*D*, *t* and *n* become soft (as if followed by a quick *y*) before *i*, *í* and *ě*. In writing they stay *d t n*; the vowel tells you:

| Spelling | Sounds like |
| --- | --- |
| *dítě* (child) | *dyee-tye* |
| *tisíc* (thousand) | *tyi-seets* |
| *nic* (nothing) | *nyits* |
| *děkuji* (thank you) | *dye-ku-yi* |

*Y* and *ý* leave them hard: *ty* (you) is hard, while *ti* (those) is soft. Many loanwords keep a hard consonant even before *i*, for example *diplom*, *titul*, *student*.

### The letter ě

*Ě* tells you the consonant before it is soft, and it is pronounced *ye* after *d*, *t*, *n*: *dělat* (to do) is *dye-lat*. After *b*, *p*, *v*, *f* it sounds like *bye*, *pye*, *vye*, *fye* (*pět* five, *věc* thing), and *mě* sounds like *mnye*. After other consonants you write a plain *e*, not *ě*.

## Stress

Stress is always on the **first syllable**. A long vowel does not take the stress, it is simply held longer. A one-syllable preposition joins the next word and carries the stress itself, so *do kina* is said *DOkina*, *v Praze* is said *VPRAze*.

## Final and neighbouring consonants

At the end of a word, voiced consonants are said unvoiced: *hrad* sounds like *hrat*, *led* like *let*. Consonant groups also adjust to each other, so *v* before a voiceless sound turns into *f*. You do not need to write any of this differently, but listen for it.

## Consonants as vowels

*R* and *l* can act as vowels, so some words have none at all: *prst* (finger), *vlk* (wolf), *krk* (neck), *Brno*, *čtvrtek* (Thursday). The classic tongue twister is *Strč prst skrz krk* (stick a finger through your throat).

## Diphthongs

*Ou* is a real diphthong, said like *oh-oo*: *dobrou noc*. *Au* and *eu* occur mostly in loanwords: *auto*, *euro*.

## Alphabetical order

*Ch* comes after *h*. Letters with a háček come right after their plain letter: *c č*, *r ř*, *s š*, *z ž*. Vowel length does not affect the order.

## Typing Czech

On a Czech keyboard layout the letters have their own keys. On other keyboards, add the Czech layout in your system settings, hold a letter on a phone keyboard to see its variants, or use the key row under the answer box in [the exercises](/practice/).
