# Past and future

## The past tense

The Czech past tense has only one form and no distinction between "I did", "I was doing" and "I have done". Aspect (see [aspect](/grammar/aspect)) carries that difference.

It is made from two parts:

1. the **l-participle**, the infinitive with *-t* replaced by *-l* (*dělat → dělal*)
2. the present tense of **být** as an auxiliary (*jsem, jsi, jsme, jste*), which is dropped in the third person

The participle agrees with the subject's **gender and number**, so a man and a woman say different things:

| | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| já | dělal jsem | dělala jsem | |
| ty | dělal jsi | dělala jsi | |
| on, ona, ono | dělal | dělala | dělalo |
| my | dělali jsme | dělaly jsme | |
| vy | dělali jste | dělaly jste | |
| oni, ony, ona | dělali | dělaly | dělala |

In the plural, **-li** is for masculine animate subjects (or a mixed group), **-ly** for feminine and masculine inanimate ones, and **-la** for neuter.

### Polite *vy*

When you address one person as *vy*, use the singular participle with the plural auxiliary: *Byl jste doma?* (to a man), *Byla jste doma?* (to a woman). Addressing several people: *Byli jste doma?*

### Where *jsem* goes

The auxiliary is a clitic and takes the second position: *Včera jsem byl doma.* or *Byl jsem doma.* It is not placed at the start of the sentence. For negation, *ne-* attaches to the participle: *Nebyl jsem doma.*

## Irregular participles

Most are regular, so the participle of *dělat* is *dělal*. These are the ones you need early:

| Infinitive | Participle (m.) | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| být | byl | to be |
| mít | měl | to have |
| jít | šel (šla, šlo; šli) | to go |
| jet | jel | to go (by vehicle) |
| přijít | přišel | to come |
| chtít | chtěl | to want |
| moct | mohl | can |
| psát | psal | to write |
| číst | četl | to read |
| jíst | jedl | to eat |
| pít | pil | to drink |
| dát | dal | to give |
| vzít | vzal | to take |
| říct | řekl | to say |

## The future tense

### Imperfective verbs: *budu* + infinitive

| já | budu dělat |
| --- | --- |
| ty | budeš dělat |
| on, ona, ono | bude dělat |
| my | budeme dělat |
| vy | budete dělat |
| oni | budou dělat |

*Zítra budu číst knihu.* Tomorrow I'll be reading a book.

### Perfective verbs: the present form means the future

A perfective verb has no real present tense. Its present-tense form talks about the future: *Zítra napíšu e-mail.* (Tomorrow I'll write an e-mail.) Never combine *budu* with a perfective infinitive.

### Verbs of motion

*Jít* and *jet* form the future with a prefix instead of *budu*: *půjdu* (I will go), *pojedu* (I will travel). *Zítra půjdu do školy.*

## Time expressions

| Czech | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| včera, dnes, zítra | yesterday, today, tomorrow |
| minulý týden, příští týden | last week, next week |
| před týdnem | a week ago |
| za týden | in a week |
| každý den, často, někdy | every day, often, sometimes |

*Před* takes the instrumental (*před týdnem*), and *za* in the sense "in" takes the accusative (*za týden*).
