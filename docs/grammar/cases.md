# The seven cases

A case is a form a noun, adjective or pronoun takes to show its job in the sentence. English does this with word order and prepositions (*the dog bites the man*, *a gift for him*). Czech does it with endings, so word order is freer and the endings matter a lot.

Czech people number the cases 1 to 7 and say things like "the sixth case". Each has a question word that helps you find it.

| # | Case | Questions | Main job | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Nominative (*nominativ*) | *kdo? co?* | The subject, the dictionary form | *Žena čte.* The woman reads. |
| 2 | Genitive (*genitiv*) | *koho? čeho?* | "of", absence, many prepositions | *Bez cukru.* Without sugar. |
| 3 | Dative (*dativ*) | *komu? čemu?* | The one something is given or said to | *Dávám dárek matce.* I give a gift to my mother. |
| 4 | Accusative (*akuzativ*) | *koho? co?* | The direct object | *Vidím ženu.* I see the woman. |
| 5 | Vocative (*vokativ*) | none | Calling someone | *Ahoj, Petře!* Hi, Petr! |
| 6 | Locative (*lokál*) | *o kom? o čem?* | Only after prepositions: place, topic | *Bydlím v Praze.* I live in Prague. |
| 7 | Instrumental (*instrumentál*) | *s kým? s čím?* | "with", the tool, the means | *Jedu s kamarádem.* I'm going with a friend. |

## One sentence, four cases

> *Matka dává bratrovi knihu pro sestru.*
> The mother gives a book to her brother for her sister.

- *matka* is the subject, nominative.
- *bratrovi* is who receives it, dative.
- *knihu* is what is given, accusative.
- *pro sestru* uses *pro*, which takes the accusative.

Because each word wears its job, you can say *Knihu dává matka bratrovi* and mean the same thing with a different emphasis.

## How to decide which case

Ask in this order:

1. **Is there a preposition?** It decides the case, and sometimes the meaning changes with the case. See [prepositions](/grammar/prepositions).
2. **Does the verb demand a case?** Many do. See the list below.
3. **Otherwise**, subject is nominative, direct object is accusative, "to or for someone" is dative, "with or by means of" is instrumental, "of" is genitive.

## Verbs that demand a case

| Case | Verb | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accusative | *mít*, *chtít*, *potřebovat*, *vidět*, *znát*, *číst*, *kupovat* | *Chci kávu.* I want a coffee. |
| Dative | *pomáhat*, *děkovat*, *věřit*, *rozumět*, *líbit se*, *patřit* | *Pomáhám bratrovi.* I help my brother. |
| Genitive | *bát se*, *ptát se*, *účastnit se* | *Bojím se psa.* I'm afraid of the dog. |
| Instrumental | *setkat se s*, *zabývat se*, *být* (as a role) | *Chci být lékařem.* I want to be a doctor. |

*Líbit se* works backwards from English. The thing you like is the subject and the person is in the dative: *Líbí se mi Praha.* (Prague is pleasing to me, so I like Prague.)

## The animate rule

For masculine **animate** nouns the accusative looks like the genitive. For inanimate ones it looks like the nominative.

| | Nominative | Accusative |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Animate | *bratr* | *Vidím bratr**a**.* |
| Inanimate | *autobus* | *Čekám na autobus.* |

## Singular or plural, same cases

All seven cases exist in the plural too, and the plural is more regular than the singular. The endings follow the noun's gender and softness: dative *-ům*, *-ám* or *-ím*; locative *-ech*, *-ách* or *-ích*; instrumental *-y*, *-ami* or *-i*. See [declension patterns](/grammar/declension) for the full tables.
