# Gender and number

Every Czech noun has a **gender** and is **singular** or **plural**. Adjectives, pronouns and past-tense verbs all agree with the noun, so you need the gender before almost anything else.

## Three genders, four kinds of noun

Masculine nouns split in two: **animate** (living beings such as people and animals) and **inanimate** (things). The split matters because they behave differently in the accusative case and in the plural.

| Gender | Looks like (nominative) | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Masculine animate | usually ends in a consonant | *pán* (gentleman), *student*, *pes* (dog) |
| Masculine inanimate | usually ends in a consonant | *hrad* (castle), *dům* (house), *stůl* (table) |
| Feminine | ends in *-a*, *-e*, or a consonant | *žena* (woman), *růže* (rose), *kost* (bone) |
| Neuter | ends in *-o*, *-e/-ě*, *-í*, or *-um* | *město* (town), *moře* (sea), *stavení* (building), *muzeum* |

::: warning A few exceptions to learn
Some masculine animate nouns end in *-a* or *-e* and still take masculine agreement: *táta* (dad), *kolega* (colleague), *soudce* (judge). *Kdo?* (who) is also masculine: *Kdo to byl?*
:::

### Telling masculine from feminine when it ends in a consonant

A consonant ending can be masculine or feminine, so learn the gender together with the word. Most consonant-final nouns are masculine. The feminine ones include abstract nouns in *-ost* (*radost* joy, *možnost* possibility) and a set of everyday words such as *kost* (bone), *věc* (thing), *noc* (night), *sůl* (salt), *píseň* (song) and *mrkev* (carrot).

## Nominative plural

| Type | Singular | Plural |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Masculine animate, hard | pán | pán**i** (also pán**ové**) |
| Masculine animate, soft | muž | muž**i** |
| Masculine inanimate, hard | hrad | hrad**y** |
| Masculine inanimate, soft | stroj | stroj**e** |
| Feminine, hard (-a) | žena | žen**y** |
| Feminine, soft (-e) | růže | růž**e** |
| Feminine (consonant) | kost | kost**i** |
| Neuter, hard (-o) | město | měst**a** |
| Neuter, soft (-e) | moře | moř**e** |

Before *-i* the consonant often changes: *student → studenti*, *Čech → Češi*, *kluk → kluci*, *dobrý chlapec → dobří chlapci*. Use the exercise sets to get a feel for them rather than memorising rules.

## Agreement in one picture

The adjective, the demonstrative and the verb all take the noun's gender and number:

- *ten nový dům* (m.), *ta nová kniha* (f.), *to nové auto* (n.)
- *Ona byla doma.* (she was at home), *Byl jsem doma.* (a man says "I was at home"), *Byla jsem doma.* (a woman says it)

That last pair is worth noticing: in the past tense the verb shows the **speaker's** gender. See [past and future](/grammar/verbs-past-future).
